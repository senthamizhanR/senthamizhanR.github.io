Research profile

Collective dynamics, with structure.

The work combines analytical modelling, large-scale numerical simulation and data-driven diagnostics to understand how interacting oscillators organize in phase and space.

I study mobile phase oscillators whose internal states synchronize while their spatial positions self-organize.

Recent work focuses on swarmalator populations with second-order harmonics, phase-lag frustration, frequency-weighted interactions, abrupt/hysteretic transitions and machine-learning assisted state-boundary detection.

order parametersRK simulationself-consistencyU-Net segmentation
01 / Higher harmonics

Second-order harmonic phase interactions in swarmalators

Explored clustered π states, mixed π states, active asynchronous states and transitions between static asynchronous and active phase-wave regimes using numerical, analytical and data-driven tools.

02 / Frustration

Frustration-induced chimeras and collective motion

Studied phase-lag effects in two-dimensional swarmalators, identifying static and active chimeras, global translational motion and synchronized global translational motion under frustration.

03 / Heterogeneity

Frequency-weighted swarmalator interactions

Analyzed a solvable one-dimensional ring model where spatial and phase couplings are scaled by intrinsic frequencies, revealing asynchronous, phase-wave and bistrip mixed states.

04 / Computation

Data-driven nonlinear dynamics

Uses computational experiments, image-based classification and reduced diagnostics to map complex phase diagrams and quantify stability boundaries in nonlinear oscillator systems.
