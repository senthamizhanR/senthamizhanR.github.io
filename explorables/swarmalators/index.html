Swarmalators with second-order harmonics

Swarmalators move in the plane and carry an internal phase. Their phases attract their positions, and their positions shape how their phases synchronise. With the second harmonic , agents can lock half a cycle apart, which produces seven collective states. Pick a state, or drag and and watch the swarm reorganise.

The model

What is being simulated

The first equation is O'Keeffe's swarmalator model: a long-range attraction that is stronger between agents with similar phases when J > 0, and a short-range repulsion that keeps them apart. The second equation couples the phases through the second harmonic, weighted by distance. Because vanishes at both and , agents can lock in anti-phase as well as in phase.

The order parameters measure how the phase is correlated with the spatial angle , taken here around the centroid of the swarm. s₁± picks up phase waves, which wind once around the swarm. s₂± picks up the doubled winding that π-locked groups produce. The browser integrates the equations with fourth-order Runge–Kutta (Δt = 0.05), with natural frequencies and self-propulsion set to zero, as in the paper.
